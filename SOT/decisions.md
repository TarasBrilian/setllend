| D39 | 2026-11-03 | **Interface v0.4: clarifications from implementing phase 2 (oracle, liquidation, bad debt).** (1) A `match_orders` batch with a `borrow` ask makes one oracle call for the debt feed and every collateral feed. In the callback a `borrow` fill without usable prices is skipped with the new reason `no_price`, and `sell_note` fills in the same batch still settle. (2) The contract validates every price itself: positive, `conf / price ≤ oracle_max_conf`, and `publish_time` within `oracle_max_age` of the block time in either direction (as Pyth checks it), on top of the oracle's own age filter. (3) Seized collateral is valued at the oracle mid price (no `conf`, as in Aave); `can_open` and the health factor stay conservative. (4) The `liquidate` and `withdraw_collateral` callbacks panic when the action no longer qualifies (no debt, healthy and not overdue, no usable price, above max LTV after withdrawal, overdue), so the keeper is refunded in full and the relayer sees the reason in the failed receipt. A liquidation that would seize 0 panics too. (5) Overflow in a callback still panics instead of skipping: fill principal is bounded by escrowed USDC, and a liquidation can be split into several calls. Overdue is checked before health, so an overflowing health check can't block settlement after `maturity + grace` | (1) One stale feed must not block note sales, which need no price, and one oracle call is cheaper than one per fill. (2) The oracle's source is private ([oracle.md](oracle.md)), so its age filter is not relied on, and a reporter clock running ahead must not keep a price fresh. (3) The mid price is the market's best estimate; valuing seizure at `price − conf` would take up to 1% more of the borrower's collateral. (4) Keeps the rule that invalid input panics with a full refund, and gives a readable reason. (5) The overflow sizes (about $300M per liquidation, about $1B of debt face per fill) can't be reached with real escrow. Details in [contract-interface.md](contract-interface.md) |
